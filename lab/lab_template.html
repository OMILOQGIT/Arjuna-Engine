Read this before quoting a number

This toy space has only 3,136 arrangements. Without a budget, a few hundred random bitstrings already cover all of it and SQD becomes trivially exact.

So we compare at a fixed budget. SQD's value is choosing the right arrangements; the quantum advantage regime is spaces far too large to cover, like IBM's 58–77-qubit SQD runs.

What is simulated, honestly

Molecule: N₂ in the STO-3G basis. CAS(6e,6o) active space on frozen-core Hartree–Fock orbitals; full valence (10e,8o) for the SQD demo.

Quantum part: exact simulation of the circuits a quantum chip would run. Tapered to 7 qubits (11 for full valence).

Noise model: depolarising errors on every gate plus readout flips, at a gate error you choose. Real chips also have crosstalk and drift.

Gate counts: one CNOT ladder per Pauli string with all-to-all wiring, so they are upper bounds on the ansatz.

Known limit: at 2.4 Å and 3.0 Å our orbital tracking loses one symmetry, so those points taper to 9–10 qubits instead of 7.

Data: integrals, Hamiltonians and ADAPT circuits come from scripts/export_lab.py; reference values from the Python test suite.
