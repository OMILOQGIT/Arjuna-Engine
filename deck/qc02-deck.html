The problem strong correlation

Stretch a bond and one electron configuration stops being enough

The N≡N triple bond is the hardest bond in chemistry to break, and breaking it is what fertiliser catalysts do. As it stretches, the Hartree–Fock determinant loses its grip on the true wavefunction.

At 2.1 Å the leading configuration carries only 19.6% of the wavefunction. Methods built on one reference start to fail.

Gate 7 sample-based quantum diagonalisation

Same noisy circuit, used differently: 0.30 mHa instead of 1.3 Ha off

SQD, 4 operators · 192 CXSQD, 8 operators · 512 CXNoisy VQE + readout fix

SQD reads bitstrings from the circuit, repairs wrong electron counts, and diagonalises H in the sampled determinants. Noise changes which determinants appear, not the energy of the ones found.

Full valence, 11 qubits, 2.1 Å, where CCSD(T) does not converge. With 3,136 determinants in total, very large shot counts cover the whole space, so the signal is the low-shot regime. IBM runs this method at 50–100+ qubits.
